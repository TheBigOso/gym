# 4 Cone Single Foot Lateral Hops: research

Researched 2026-11-26 for issue #50 (Batch 1, exercise 10 of 10). Movement source: GymVisual #906712 (see `reference.md`). Stand on one leg beside a line of four small cones. Hop sideways over each cone in turn, landing softly on the same foot and sticking briefly before the next hop.

**Evidence tags.** [trial evidence] means a peer-reviewed study measured it. [clinical opinion] means a clinical guideline, protocol or expert view, or our own reasoning. **Where the evidence is thin:** no study tests this exact drill. The closest research is on the clinical **side hop test**, which uses two lines 30 to 40 cm apart and continuous back-and-forth hopping, not four cones. Most findings below are therefore indirect. Muscle data come from repeated lateral hopping and lateral hop landings, not cone hops.

## Exercise card

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Plyometric / agility (GymVisual lists it as "Aerobic"). Also used in knee and ankle rehab and return-to-sport hopping progressions. [clinical opinion] |
| Body part | Legs (hopping leg): calves/ankle, quadriceps, glutes; hip abductors and core as stabilizers |
| Equipment | Body weight plus 4 small cones (about 15 cm tall, or flat markers to start). Spacing 30 to 40 cm between cones, matching side hop test lines (30 cm in ankle protocols, 40 cm in ACL protocols). Start narrow and short; wider and taller cones raise the load. [clinical opinion; spacing anchored to the test protocols below] |
| Difficulty | Intermediate to advanced. Davies et al. rate medial-lateral jumps intermediate and single-leg hops advanced. [clinical opinion] |
| Mechanics | Compound, plyometric (stretch-shortening cycle) |
| Plane | Frontal (lateral travel); landings also load the sagittal plane (knee and hip flexion) |
| Laterality | Unilateral. One leg hops down the line of cones and back, then switch legs. |

### Muscles

| Role | Muscles (hopping leg) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Calves (soleus, gastrocnemius), quadriceps, gluteus maximus | Soleus activation rose with faster lateral hopping [trial evidence: Suwanmana 2026]. The quads absorb landing through knee flexion (knee flexion moment measured in side hop landings) [trial evidence: Markström 2020]. Glute max was active before lateral hop landings [trial evidence: Webster 2016]. |
| Synergist | Hamstrings, tibialis anterior, hip adductors (push-off toward the far side) | Tibialis anterior activity was measured and changed with hop speed [trial evidence: Suwanmana 2026]. Hamstring and adductor roles are inferred [clinical opinion]. |
| Stabilizer | Hip abductors (gluteus medius, TFL), peroneals (peroneus longus/brevis), deep hip rotators, trunk/obliques | Peroneus longus was active in repeated lateral hopping [trial evidence: Suwanmana 2026]. Pre-landing peroneus longus activity was higher in people with chronic ankle instability (CAI) [trial evidence: Webster 2016]. Gluteus medius activity moderately correlated with hip adduction angle in side landings [trial evidence, thesis: Neamatallah 2018]. Gluteus medius did not differ between CAI and controls [trial evidence: Webster 2016]. |

Wider lateral hops raised frontal-plane ankle motion and peak eversion moment, which is the peroneals' job, but muscle activation did not change with distance [trial evidence: Suwanmana 2026]. Treat cone spacing as an ankle-loading dial, not a muscle-activation dial.

### Landing norms

| Measure | Norm | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Knee flexion at initial contact | About 30 to 37 degrees in side hop landings. Controls averaged 30.0 degrees; ACL-reconstructed knees 37.2 degrees. | [trial evidence: Markström 2020] |
| Peak knee flexion | About 59 to 63 degrees in side hop landings | [trial evidence: Markström 2020] |
| Knee flexion in general landings | LESS screen: more than 30 degrees at contact, then more than 45 degrees of further flexion. Stiff landings score as errors. | [clinical opinion: MedStar LESS sheet; this is a two-leg jump-landing screen, so indirect] |
| Frontal-plane knee control | Knee stays over the midfoot. Any medial knee collapse (dynamic valgus) is a fault. Peak knee abduction in side hop landings was only about 3 degrees in both ACL and control knees. The side hop loads frontal-plane knee control more than forward or vertical hops. | [trial evidence: Markström 2020; Sports Biomech 2021 side hop comparison]; [clinical opinion: MedStar LESS; Kansas Health System counts dynamic valgus as a failed hop] |
| Stick time | 2 to 3 s still on the landing leg in clinical hop-for-distance testing. The side hop test is continuous and has no hold. For this drill, a brief stick of about 1 to 2 s between cones fits the GymVisual reference. No study sets a stick time for cone hops. | [clinical opinion: Region Kalmar hop test guideline]; the drill's 1 to 2 s is our choice |
| Faults that void a hop | Fall, other foot touching down, extra short hop on landing, foot touching the line or cone, arm touch-down | [clinical opinion: Kansas Health System; Region Kalmar; Physiotutors] |
| Symmetry | Side-to-side difference under 10% (LSI of 90% or more) is the usual pass mark | [trial evidence: Gustavsson 2006; Piussi 2020]. But good LSI can hide poor knee mechanics [trial evidence: Welling 2018; Kotsifaki 2021, both on forward hops]. |

### Breathing

Breathe naturally and rhythmically. Exhale lightly on take-off or landing, and take a quick breath during each stick. Brace the trunk without holding your breath through all four hops. [clinical opinion: we found no study on breathing during lateral hopping]

### Prescription

- **Sets/reps:** 3 to 4 sets per leg. Each set is one pass down the four cones and back (8 hops), resting 60 to 90 s between sets. [clinical opinion]
- **Volume cap:** Count foot contacts. Davies et al. suggest 80 to 100 contacts per session for beginners, up to 120 to 140 for advanced athletes. [clinical opinion; the authors say most plyometric guidance is empirical]
- **Frequency:** 2 sessions per week. Allow 48 to 72 h between high-intensity lower-body plyometric sessions. [clinical opinion: Davies 2015]
- **Progression:** Two-leg lateral hops, then single-leg hops over a floor line, then flat markers, then small cones with a stick, then faster continuous hops with no stick, then wider spacing or taller cones. [clinical opinion: Davies 2015 low-to-high progression; Suwanmana 2026 shows distance raises ankle frontal-plane load]
- **Prerequisites (rehab):** No pain, swelling or range-of-motion limits. 30 s single-leg balance. Strength within about 20% of the other side. [clinical opinion: Davies 2015 table 1]. A Swedish ACL guideline adds roughly 85 to 90% strength symmetry and some prior hop training before side hop testing [clinical opinion: Region Kalmar].

### Cues

1. Stand tall on one leg with the knee soft and the hips level. Keep the free foot off the floor beside or behind you.
2. Push off from the ball of the foot and hop just high and wide enough to clear the cone.
3. Land quietly on the ball of the foot, then let the heel settle as the knee and hip bend.
4. Keep the knee over the middle of the foot: no caving inward.
5. Stick the landing for a beat, hips level and chest up, before the next hop.
6. Eyes forward, not down at the cones. Use the arms lightly for balance.
7. Hop down the line and back, then switch legs.

[clinical opinion, consistent with the landing norms above]

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Knee caves inward on landing (dynamic valgus) | Shrink the hop or the spacing. Think "knee over the second toe" and push the knee slightly out. Strengthen the hip abductors. [clinical opinion; valgus is a failed hop in the Kansas protocol] |
| Stiff, loud landing with a nearly straight knee | Land on the forefoot, then bend into about 30 degrees or more of knee flexion at contact. [trial evidence for the norm: Markström 2020; clinical opinion for the cue] |
| Hip drops or trunk leans far over the landing leg | Keep the pelvis level and the chest over the foot. Lower the cone height until you can. [clinical opinion; ACL-reconstructed limbs used more trunk and hip flexion in side hops: Markström 2020] |
| Extra stutter hop or the free foot touching down | Slow down and earn a full 1 to 2 s stick before the next hop. [clinical opinion: Kansas Health System and Region Kalmar count these as failed hops] |
| Rolling onto the outside of the foot (ankle inversion) | Land on the ball of the foot with the foot pointing forward. Narrow the spacing. Stop if the ankle feels unstable. [clinical opinion; wider hops raise ankle frontal-plane load: Suwanmana 2026] |
| Clipping cones by hopping too low or watching your feet | Look ahead, lift the knee and drive from the ankle. Start with flat markers. [clinical opinion] |
| Racing through reps as form fades | Stop the set when technique declines. [clinical opinion: Davies 2015] |

### Safety / who should modify

- **Recent ankle sprain or chronic ankle instability:** Side hop performance discriminates CAI [trial evidence: Docherty 2005; Rosen 2017 meta-analysis, low-quality studies]. So this drill is demanding for an unstable ankle. Start with floor lines and narrow spacing, and progress only without giving way. [clinical opinion]
- **After ACL reconstruction or knee surgery:** Use the drill only once cleared for hopping, usually late rehab. Clinicians gate side hop testing on strength and prior hop training [clinical opinion: Region Kalmar]. Passing LSI alone does not prove good mechanics [trial evidence: Welling 2018; Kotsifaki 2021].
- **Contraindications:** Pain, acute sprain or strain, joint effusion, unhealed ligament, cartilage or bone injury, and insufficient base strength. Davies calls insufficient base strength probably the biggest contraindication. [clinical opinion: Davies 2015]
- **Others who should modify:** Beginners, older adults, people with poor single-leg balance, and heavier individuals. Use two-leg lateral hops or single-leg balance work first. [clinical opinion]
- **Surface:** Use a firm, non-slip floor or a sprung gym floor. Cones should be soft and collapsible so a clipped cone does not trip the athlete. [clinical opinion]

## 1. Scientific studies

### Side hop test and ACL return to sport

- [Gustavsson et al. 2006, A test battery for evaluating hop performance after ACL injury and reconstruction (KSSTA)](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00167-006-0045-6). Chose the side hop as the test of hopping while fatigued. ICCs for the hop tests were 0.85 to 0.97. "Normal" symmetry was set at under 10% side-to-side difference. [trial evidence]
- [Piussi et al. 2020, Hop tests and psychological PROs as an RTS battery after ACLR (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7218571). The side hop is hopping over 2 lines 40 cm apart for 30 s, passing at LSI of 90% or more. Only 13% passed the full battery with psychological measures. [trial evidence]
- [Mirković et al. 2022, Evaluation of the limb symmetry index: the side hop test (Front Physiol)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.874632/full). 15 healthy men hopping over 30 cm lines. LSI stayed stable (about 1.00 to 1.06) while speed improved with practice. [trial evidence; small sample]
- [Markström et al. 2020, Side-hop landings after ACL reconstruction: movement strategies and knee robustness (Am J Sports Med)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7163247). Knee flexion at contact was about 30 degrees in controls and 37 in ACLR knees, peaking at about 59 to 63 degrees. Knee abduction was small and did not differ. ACLR limbs used more hip and trunk flexion. [trial evidence]
- [Standardised rebound side hop vs. hop for distance and vertical hop after ACLR (Sports Biomechanics, DOI 10.1080/14763141.2020.1869296)](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14763141.2020.1869296). The side hop produced greater joint angles and moments, especially in the frontal plane. The authors favour it for judging knee landing control. [trial evidence; author names not shown on the fetched page]
- [Welling et al. 2018, Altered movement during single leg hop test after ACLR (KSSTA)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00167-018-4893-7.pdf). Patients who passed LSI of 90% still showed reduced peak knee flexion on the injured side. This is a forward hop, so indirect. [trial evidence]
- [Kotsifaki et al. 2021, Single leg hop for distance symmetry masks lower limb biomechanics (BJSM)](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/614858). 97% distance LSI but only 69% knee-work symmetry. The authors write "the distance hopped is a poor measure of knee function". Forward hop, so indirect. [trial evidence]

### Ankle instability and lateral hopping

- [Docherty et al. 2005, Functional-performance deficits in volunteers with functional ankle instability (J Athl Train, PMID 15902321)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15902321/). Side hop time correlated with the instability index (r = .35). Single hop for distance did not. [trial evidence; metadata verified through the Europe PMC record because PubMed returned a browser check]
- [Buchanan, Docherty & Schrader 2008, Functional performance testing in functional ankle instability (J Athl Train, PMID 18668180)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18668180/). Single-limb hopping deficits appeared only in those who felt instability during testing. [trial evidence; verified through Europe PMC as above]
- [Rosen, Needle & Ko 2017, Functional performance tests to identify chronic ankle instability: systematic review and meta-analysis (Clin J Sport Med)](https://phes.appstate.edu/sites/default/files/rosen_needle_functional_tests_ankle.pdf). The side hop and timed hop tests had the best clinical utility for spotting CAI. Caveats: heterogeneity, low study quality and one influential outlier study. The text gives the side hop distance as "3-cm", probably meaning 30 cm. [trial evidence, low quality]
- [Webster, Pietrosimone & Gribble 2016, Muscle activation during lateral hop landing before and after fatigue with or without CAI (J Athl Train)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5094841). Pre-landing peroneus longus and glute max activity was higher in CAI. Gluteus medius did not differ. [trial evidence; 16 per group]
- [Suwanmana et al. 2026, Ankle biomechanics and muscle activation during repeated single-leg lateral hopping (Biomechanics, MDPI)](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/29190/1/Ankle%20Biomechanics%20and%20Muscle%20Activation%20During%20Repeated%20Single%20Leg%20Lateral%20Hopping%20Effects%20of%20Speed%20and%20Distance.pdf). Measured peroneus longus, tibialis anterior, lateral gastrocnemius and soleus. Wider (45 cm) hops raised ankle frontal-plane range of motion and eversion moment, but distance did not change muscle activation. Faster hops raised soleus activation. [trial evidence; the most direct study for this drill]

### Hip control in single-leg landing

- [Neamatallah 2018, Do the gluteal muscles influence dynamic knee valgus when single-leg landing? (PhD thesis, University of Salford)](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1491722). Gluteus medius EMG moderately correlated with hip adduction in forward, medial and lateral single-leg landings (R squared 0.13 to 0.22). Links were task-, limb- and sex-dependent. [trial evidence, thesis; moderate effects only]

### Plyometric progression

- [Davies, Riemann & Manske 2015, Current concepts of plyometric exercise (IJSPT)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4637913). Covers low-to-high progression, foot-contact volumes, 48 to 72 h recovery, prerequisites and contraindications, and stopping when technique fades. The authors note most guidance is empirical. [clinical opinion, narrative review]

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

- [Physiotutors: Hop test cluster (side hop protocol)](https://www.physiotutors.com/wiki/hop-test-cluster/). Lines 40 cm apart, 30 s, hands behind the back. Touching the tape is an error. Repeat the trial if more than 25% of hops are errors. [clinical opinion]
- [Region Kalmar (Sweden): side hop test instructions, Swedish PDF](https://vardgivare.regionkalmar.se/globalassets/vard-och-behandling/rehabilitering-och-habilitering/rehabilitering/riktlinjer/fysioterapi/kna/bilaga-sidohoppstest.pdf). 40 cm, 30 s, best of 2 trials. Test only after about 85 to 90% strength and some hop training. [clinical opinion]
- [Region Kalmar (Sweden): knee hop test battery, Swedish PDF](https://vardgivare.regionkalmar.se/globalassets/vard-och-behandling/rehabilitering-och-habilitering/rehabilitering/riktlinjer/fysioterapi/kna/bilaga-hopptester.pdf). Hold the landing for 2 to 3 s on distance and vertical hops. Good knee control is required. Target 90% of the uninjured side before return to sport. [clinical opinion]
- [The University of Kansas Health System: ankle functional testing protocol (PDF)](https://www.kansashealthsystem.com/-/media/project/website/pdfs-for-download/pt-protocols/functional-testing-protocolankle.pdf). Side hop over 30 cm, 10 round trips for time, LSI of 90% or more. Falls, extra hops and dynamic valgus fail the attempt. Cites Caffrey et al. 2009 (JOSPT). [clinical opinion]
- [MedStar Health: Landing Error Scoring System (LESS) sheet (PDF)](https://www.medstarhealth.org/-/media/project/mho/medstar/services/pdf/less-landing-error-scoring-system.pdf). Knee flexion over 30 degrees at contact and over 45 degrees of displacement. Knees over the midfoot. Soft rather than stiff landing. This is a two-leg landing screen, so indirect. [clinical opinion]
- [Physiopedia: Hop tests](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Hop_tests). Single, triple, crossover and 6 m timed hops. Land firmly without losing balance. Under 10% limb difference. Judge landing quality alongside scores. [clinical opinion]
- [Physiopedia: Hop test](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Hop_Test). Companion page with the same four-test battery and 90% threshold. [clinical opinion]

**Gaps and limits.** We found no NSCA article on lateral cone hops that we could verify, and no study of breathing, cone height or stick time for this drill. We could not fetch the Caffrey et al. 2009 JOSPT paper on functional ankle instability; it is cited here only through the Kansas protocol. Several PubMed/PMC pages returned browser checks during research. The two PubMed-linked ankle papers were verified through their Europe PMC records instead.
