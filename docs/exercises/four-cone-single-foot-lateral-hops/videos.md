## Pattern clarification

- **Cone count and layout:** four small cones in a straight line, side-on to the athlete (GymVisual). The closest coach videos use three mini-hurdles in a line (Next Level Rochester, BEFORM PT).
- **Cone spacing and height:** none of the sources give numbers. Suggested for the animation (our inference, not sourced): low disc or mini cones about 15-23 cm (6-9 in) tall, spaced about 45-60 cm (18-24 in) apart. That is roughly one comfortable single-leg lateral hop, so each landing falls midway between two cones.
- **Stick or continuous:** GymVisual's description ("landing softly ... sticking briefly, then the next hop") matches the stick version: land, hold for about 1 second, then hop again (Next Level Rochester; Champion PT cues 1-2 s). BEFORM PT shows the continuous, reactive progression. Animate the stick version.
- **Return direction:** hop laterally down the line over all four cones, then hop back over them in the opposite direction on the same leg. Next Level Rochester ("over them laterally and back") and Live Lean TV (hop back to the start) both support this. GymVisual's 35 s clip length is consistent with out-and-back on one leg, then the other leg.
- **When legs switch:** after a full set on one leg (all cones out and back, or all reps), never mid-line. Next Level Rochester and Live Lean TV both describe finishing on one leg, then switching.
- **Stance and landing:** the free leg is bent with the foot lifted behind or beside the support leg. Land on the ball of the foot into a soft bend at the hip and knee, with the knee tracking over the toes (no inward knee collapse), and use the arms for drive and balance.

## Confidence

Medium for the pattern (line of small obstacles, same-leg hops with a soft stuck landing, out-and-back, switch legs after the set). Several coach and physical therapy sources agree on this, but none shows exactly four cones. Low for cone spacing and height, which no source states (the numbers above are suggested defaults).
