# 1 to 2 Jump Box: research

GymVisual reference #420913 (see `reference.md`). The athlete takes off from **one leg** and lands on **both feet** on top of a plyo box, then steps down.

**Evidence tags.** [trial evidence] means a peer-reviewed study measured it. [clinical opinion] means a professional body, clinician or coach says so but no trial tests it. **No study we could verify measured this exact exercise** (single-leg takeoff onto a box with a two-foot landing). The numbers below come from nearby tasks: drop landings from 30-31 cm boxes, single-leg vs two-leg jumps and landings, and plyometric training trials. Treat them as indirect.

## Exercise card

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Plyometric / lower-body power |
| Body part | Legs (quadriceps, glutes, calves) |
| Secondary | Core, hamstrings, hip flexors (swing leg), shoulders (arm swing) |
| Equipment | Plyo box, foam/soft-top preferred; non-slip floor |
| Difficulty | Intermediate (a single-leg takeoff progression of the two-foot box jump) [clinical opinion: Physiopedia lists bilateral to unilateral as a progression] |
| Plane | Sagittal |
| Laterality | Unilateral takeoff, bilateral landing; alternate takeoff leg, equal reps per leg |
| Mechanics | Compound, stretch-shortening cycle |

### Muscles

- **Target:** quadriceps, gluteus maximus, gastrocnemius and soleus of the takeoff leg (knee, hip and ankle extension). [clinical opinion: NASM lists quads, glutes and calves as the main box jump muscles]
- **Synergist:** hamstrings and adductor magnus (hip extension at takeoff); hip flexors of the swing leg (drive the free knee up so both feet meet in flight); anterior deltoids (arm swing). [clinical opinion: NASM lists hip flexors and core as secondary. The specific roles are standard anatomy, not measured in this exercise]
- **Stabilizer:** gluteus medius and hip rotators (keep the takeoff knee over the foot, prevent valgus), core/trunk extensors (brace through takeoff and landing), tibialis anterior and peroneals (ankle control on the single-leg push and the landing). [clinical opinion]
- **Single-leg takeoff shifts demand toward the hip and ankle.** In repeated rebound jumps, single-leg takeoffs used relatively more hip torque and power than two-leg takeoffs. [trial evidence, conference paper, 12 male athletes, ISBS 2011]

### Joint-angle and load norms (all indirect)

| Measure | Norm | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Takeoff leg, peak force | About 3.0 x body weight for a single-leg jump vs about 2.0 x for a two-leg countermovement jump (Leissring et al.); one-leg running jumps about 3.0 x vs 1.6 x for two-leg (Tai et al. 2018) | [trial evidence, indirect] |
| Takeoff leg, stiffness | One-leg running jumps showed higher knee, hip and ankle stiffness than two-leg jumps (Tai et al. 2018) | [trial evidence, indirect] |
| Takeoff trunk | Inclined forward with arm swing (matches the reference GIF) | [clinical opinion: ACE single-leg push-off cues a slightly forward trunk] |
| Landing, knee flexion at initial contact | About 16-29 degrees in double-leg drop landings from a 30 cm box (Etnoyer et al. 2013; Li et al. 2023). Single-leg landings touched down straighter, about 9-10 degrees (Li et al. 2023) | [trial evidence, indirect] |
| Landing, peak knee flexion | About 81-96 degrees in double-leg drop landings from 30-31 cm (Etnoyer et al. 2013; Mancini et al. 2022) | [trial evidence, indirect: a drop landing falls farther than a box-top landing, so the box jump likely needs less] |
| Landing, hip flexion at peak knee flexion | About 77-86 degrees (Etnoyer et al. 2013) | [trial evidence, indirect] |
| Landing posture target for this exercise | Quarter-to-half squat, "athletic position": hips back, knees over toes, chest slightly forward. Land in about the same position you took off from. A deep squat on the box means the box is too high | [clinical opinion: Breaking Muscle, Power Athlete, reference GIF]. Animation estimate, not a measured norm: about 45-75 degrees knee flexion at the settled landing |
| Trunk lean on landing | Leaning the trunk forward in single-leg drop landings cut peak vertical force to 3.9 x body weight vs 5.3 x when upright, with more knee flexion (Shimokochi et al. 2012) | [trial evidence, indirect] |
| Box height | Beginners about 12-18 in (30-46 cm), progressing as landing mechanics improve (NASM). Reference GIF shows about knee height | [clinical opinion]. No trial compares box heights for this exercise |

**Thin evidence.** We found no study of joint angles while landing **up onto** a box. One general principle helps: a box-top landing happens near the top of the jump, so the body falls less than in a drop landing. The drop-landing peaks above are therefore probably upper bounds. This is our inference and has not been measured.

### Breathing

Inhale during the step into the stagger. Exhale sharply as you drive off the takeoff leg. Hold a brace through flight and the landing, then breathe normally as you stand and step down. [clinical opinion; none of the verified sources give a breathing pattern for box jumps]

### Prescription

- **Sets x reps:** 3-5 sets of 3-5 reps per takeoff leg; never to failure. [clinical opinion: NASM box jump guidance; per-leg split from ACE's equal-reps-per-leg rule]
- **Rest:** full rest between sets [clinical opinion: NASM]. The sources we verified give no rest time in seconds. Our app default of about 60-90 s is an unsourced placeholder.
- **Frequency:** 2-3 sessions per week, with 48-72 h recovery between plyometric sessions. [clinical opinion: Physiopedia]
- **Box height progression:** start low (30-46 cm). Raise the box only while the landing stays quiet, balanced and in a half squat. Progress from a two-foot takeoff box jump to this 1-to-2 version, then to single-leg landings. [clinical opinion: NASM, Physiopedia, Breaking Muscle; NSCA (Landow) describes going from low bilateral jumps to single-leg deceleration]
- **Do not train fatigued:** fatigue degrades landing mechanics. [clinical opinion: NASM]

### Step-by-step cues

1. Stand tall about a short step behind the box. Brace lightly and keep your arms at your sides.
2. Step into a staggered stance with the takeoff foot forward. Hinge slightly forward and draw your arms back.
3. Drive off the takeoff leg through hip, knee and ankle as both arms swing forward and up. Exhale on the drive.
4. Pull the free knee up and bring the takeoff leg to meet it, so both feet arrive together.
5. Land softly on both feet, mid-foot first, fully on the box top. Push your hips back into a quarter-to-half squat with knees tracking over your toes.
6. Stick the landing for a beat, then stand up tall on the box.
7. Step down one foot at a time; do not jump down. Reset, then switch takeoff leg.

[clinical opinion: built from NASM, ACE single-leg push-off, BarBend and Breaking Muscle cues plus the reference GIF]

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Box too high: tucking the knees to clear the edge, landing in a deep squat | Use a lower box. Land in the same half squat you took off from | [clinical opinion: NASM, STACK, Breaking Muscle] |
| Stiff, straight-knee or loud landing | Land quietly with soft knees and hips back so the big muscles absorb the load | [clinical opinion: NASM, BarBend]. Supported indirectly: upright, stiff landings had higher peak forces [trial evidence, Shimokochi 2012] |
| Knees caving inward (valgus) on takeoff or landing | Cue "knees out" over the middle toes. Lower the box or go back to two-foot takeoffs until it is clean | [clinical opinion: BarBend, Power Athlete] |
| Toes catching the box edge, or landing half on the box | Stand close enough, jump up and slightly forward, and aim to land with whole feet on the box top | [trial evidence (injury epidemiology): box jump slips caused all 20 lower-leg wounds in Lenz et al. 2024] |
| Uneven landing: one foot lands first or weight shifts to the takeoff side | Bring the takeoff leg up to meet the swing leg in flight and land both feet at once | [clinical opinion: NASM cues balance between both feet]. Landings are more asymmetric than takeoffs [trial evidence, Cone and Lee 2021] |
| Jumping backward off the box | Step down. Jumping down turns every rep into a drop landing with extra Achilles and knee load | [clinical opinion: NASM, STACK] |
| Only training the dominant takeoff leg | Alternate legs and match reps per side | [clinical opinion: ACE] |

### Safety and who should modify

- **Lower-leg wounds from slipping** are the main reported box jump injury. In a CrossFit injury survey, box jumps had the highest isolated injury share (14.3%), and all 20 lower-leg wounds came from slipping during box jumps. The authors suggest foam boxes. [trial evidence (survey/epidemiology), Lenz et al. 2024]
- **Single-leg loading is harder on the knee.** Single-leg stop-jump and drop landings showed straighter knees at contact, higher forces and higher valgus moments than two-leg landings (Wang 2011; Li et al. 2023). In this exercise the landing is two-footed, which is the safer half. Still, the takeoff leg sees roughly 3 x body weight. [trial evidence, indirect]
- **Modify or avoid** (use a low box, a two-foot takeoff or a step-up instead) with: acute or subacute ankle/knee sprains, acute inflammation, recent surgery, joint instability, a history of stress injuries, or poor base strength. [clinical opinion: Physiopedia contraindications list]
- **Knee pain, patellar or Achilles tendinopathy, prior ACL injury:** get clinician clearance. Keep the box low and use step-downs only. [clinical opinion]
- **Visible knee valgus or stiff landings:** regress to landing drills first. Technique instruction and feedback increase landing knee flexion, often within 1-3 sessions. [trial evidence: Etnoyer et al. 2013 RCT; Bathe et al. 2023 meta-analysis]
- **Why it is worth doing:** neuromuscular training programs, which usually include plyometrics, cut ACL injuries by about half in a meta-analysis of 24 trials. Trial quality was generally low. [trial evidence, Donnell-Fink et al. 2015] A 16-week plyometric program increased landing knee flexion and lowered loading rate vs traditional strength training. [trial evidence, small RCT, Bocheng et al. 2024]

## 1. Scientific studies

### Landing mechanics and knee angles

- [Etnoyer et al. 2013, Instruction and jump-landing kinematics in college-aged female athletes (J Athl Train)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3600918). RCT, 43 women, 30 cm box-drop-jump. Expert plus self video feedback raised peak knee flexion. Gives contact and peak knee/hip angles.
- [Li, Song, Jenkins and Dai 2023, Prelanding knee kinematics and landing kinetics, single- vs double-leg landings (J Appl Biomech)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10498700). 52 recreational athletes. Single-leg landings touched down with about half the knee flexion of double-leg landings.
- [Shimokochi et al. 2012, Sagittal plane body position during single-leg landings and ACL risk (Knee Surg Sports Traumatol Arthrosc)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269904). Leaning forward lowered peak force (3.9 vs 5.3 x body weight) and knee extensor moment compared with an upright landing.
- [Mancini et al. 2022, Soccer-specific vertical jump and landing kinematics (Sports Med Health Sci)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9453686). Small study, 8 players, 31 cm box. Peak knee flexion about 90-96 degrees on the first landing.
- [Bates, Ford, Myer and Hewett 2013, First vs second landing of a drop vertical jump (J Biomech)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3644482). 205 adolescent female athletes, 31 cm box. Peak force about 4.1 x body weight on both landings; asymmetry grew on the second.
- [Harry, Silvernail, Mercer and Dufek 2018, Vertical jump landings vs step-off landings from equal heights (J Strength Cond Res)](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/kns_fac_articles/246). Step-off (drop) landings had a larger first force peak than jump landings from the same height. Indirectly relevant to stepping down rather than dropping off the box.
- [Cone and Lee 2021, Lower limb force asymmetries during landing and jumping (Int J Exerc Sci)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8136551). Pilot, 11 participants. Left-right force asymmetry was about 11-13% on landing vs about 4% on takeoff.

### Single-leg vs two-leg takeoff and landing

- [Tai, Wang and Peng 2018, One-legged vs two-legged running vertical jumps (J Hum Kinet)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6231348). 10 volleyball players. One-leg takeoff about doubled peak force and raised joint stiffness; two-leg jumps went higher.
- [The differences between double and single leg takeoff on joint kinetics during rebound-type jump (ISBS Proceedings 2011)](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4022430?lng=en). 12 track athletes. Single-leg takeoffs used relatively more hip torque and power. Conference abstract only.
- [Leissring, Petushek, Stephenson and Jensen, Ground and knee joint reaction forces in plyometric exercises (ISBS Proceedings)](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4420/4110). 18 students. Single-leg jump peak force about 3.0 x body weight vs about 2.0 x for countermovement and drop jumps.
- [Wang 2011, Lower extremity biomechanics of single- and double-leg stop-jump tasks (J Sports Sci Med)](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-10-151.xml-Fulltext). 10 volleyball players. Single-leg landings: less hip/knee flexion, higher forces and valgus moments.
- [Zhao et al. 2024, Unilateral vs bilateral plyometric training in youth basketball (J Sports Sci Med)](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-23-647.xml-Fulltext). RCT, 75 players, 8 weeks. Both improved jumps equally; unilateral training improved single-leg landing stabilization more.

### Training effects, intensity and injury

- [Bathe et al. 2023, Training interventions to reduce lower-limb injury risk during landing, meta-analysis (BMJ Open Sport Exerc Med)](https://bmjopensem.bmj.com/content/9/2/e001508.full.pdf). 31 studies. Technique training most consistently increased knee flexion; plyometrics alone had no clear effect on landing force.
- [Donnell-Fink et al. 2015, Effectiveness of knee injury and ACL tear prevention programs, meta-analysis (PLoS ONE)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4670212). 24 trials. ACL injury rate ratio about 0.49 with neuromuscular/proprioceptive training; study quality was generally low.
- [Bocheng et al. 2024, 16 weeks of plyometric training and landing knee biomechanics (Eur J Sport Sci)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11295097). Small RCT, 24 male basketball players. More landing knee flexion and lower loading rate than traditional strength training.
- [Makaruk et al. 2014, Single vs repeated plyometrics on landing and jumping (Biol Sport)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3994579). 36 untrained men, 6 weeks, program included box jumps. Both raised jump height; only repeated jumps lowered landing force.
- [Intensity rankings of plyometric exercises using joint power absorption (Clin Biomech 2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25087112/). 10 athletes, 7 bilateral drills including box jump up. Joint-power rankings did not match the usual low/medium/high labels.
- [Lenz et al. 2024, From Sweat to Strain: training-related injuries in CrossFit (Open Access J Sports Med)](https://www.dovepress.com/from-sweat-to-strain-an-epidemiological-analysis-of-training-related-i-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-OAJSM). Box jumps had the highest isolated injury share (14.3%); all lower-leg wounds came from box jump slips.

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

### Professional bodies

- [NASM: Box jumps (exercise library)](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/box-jumps). Muscles, 12-18 in beginner box, 3-5 x 3-5, step down, never to failure, common mistakes.
- [ACE: Single leg push-off (exercise library)](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/lifestyle/exercise-library/230/single-leg-push-off). Box-based single-leg plyometric. Landing cues (mid-foot, hips back, no locked knees), equal reps per leg.
- [Physiopedia: Plyometrics](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Plyometrics). Stretch-shortening cycle phases, strength prerequisites, 48-72 h recovery, bilateral-to-unilateral progression, contraindications list.
- [NSCA: Plyometric implementation, jump landing positions to decrease injury (Loren Landow, video page)](https://www.nsca.com/education/videos/plyometric-implementation-setup-and-execution-of-jump-landing-positions-to-decrease-likelihood-of-injuries). Progression from low bilateral jumps to single-leg deceleration drills.
- [NSCA: Bridge, jump-landing for females (video page)](https://www.nsca.com/education/videos/bridge-series/bridge-jump-landing-for-females). Demonstrates box jump landing technique, common errors and a teaching progression.

### Coaching and practitioner articles

- [Human Kinetics: Power training principles, form and execution (Radcliffe, High-Powered Plyometrics excerpt)](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/power-training-principles-form-and-execution). Posture and balance checks; "toe-up" and "hip-up" landing cues.
- [Breaking Muscle: Proper plyometrics, how to box jump (Kish)](https://breakingmuscle.com/fitness/proper-plyometrics-how-to-box-jump-vertical-jump-and-broad-jump-correctly). Land in your takeoff position; a deep-squat landing means the box is too high.
- [BarBend: I know you can jump, but can you land? (Pearson)](https://barbend.com/jump-training-landing/). Soft knees, knees out, land quietly, active feet.
- [Power Athlete: Plyometric training, landing (Welbourn)](https://powerathletehq.com/plyometric-training-landing/). Athletic-position landing, hips back, knee valgus causes. Partly members-only.
- [STACK: 3 common box jump mistakes](https://www.stack.com/?p=293633). Box too high, jumping backward off the box, only doing box jumps.

**Gaps.** No study of the 1-to-2 box jump itself. No measured joint angles for landing onto a box. No trial-based box height, rest or breathing guidance. PubMed/PMC blocked several fetches with a browser check, so a few likely relevant papers could not be checked and were left out.
