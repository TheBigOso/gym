# 3 Point Standing Hops: research

Batch 1, exercise 8 of 10 (GitHub issue #38). GymVisual reference #768012; movement notes in `reference.md` in this folder. Researched 2026-11-26.

**Evidence tags.** [trial evidence] = measured in a study (often on a related task, said so where it applies). [clinical opinion] = professional guidance, expert commentary or our own reasoning for the animation. No study tests this exact drill (two-foot hops to three points around a center). Every number below comes from a related task: two-leg hopping in place, single-leg lateral hopping, multidirectional single-leg landings, or the two-foot hexagon agility test. Treat them as guide ranges, not norms for this drill.

**Direction pattern (open question for the video ticket).** The reference camera orbits and hides the direction of travel. The closest tested drill, the hexagon agility test, has the athlete face forward the whole time and hop out over a side and back to the center with feet together (Topend Sports, citing Beekhuizen 2009). A matching pattern here would be center, forward, center, side, center, back, center, facing forward throughout. [clinical opinion]

### Muscles

| Role | Muscles | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Ankle plantarflexors (soleus, gastrocnemius) | In two-leg hopping, changes in leg stiffness came mainly from the ankle, not the knee (Jessup 2025). Soleus activity rose with faster single-leg lateral hops (Suwanmana 2026). [trial evidence] |
| Target | Quadriceps | Absorb the landing through the small knee bend and help the rebound. Knee flexion during repeated two-leg hopping measured at about 20 degrees (Kovacs 2021). GymVisual highlights the quadriceps. [trial evidence for knee motion; muscle role is clinical opinion] |
| Synergist | Gluteus maximus, hamstrings | Hip extension on take-off; small at this amplitude. [clinical opinion] |
| Synergist | Hip flexors | Lift the feet clear between contacts; GymVisual highlights them. [clinical opinion] |
| Synergist | Tibialis anterior | Positions the ankle before contact; its landing activity dropped at faster hop speed in single-leg lateral hops (Suwanmana 2026). [trial evidence, single-leg task] |
| Stabilizer | Peroneals (fibularis longus) | Control side-to-side ankle motion on the lateral hop; highly active in single-leg lateral hops (Suwanmana 2026). [trial evidence, single-leg task] |
| Stabilizer | Foot intrinsics (flexor digitorum brevis, abductor hallucis) | Active during contact in two-leg hopping in place (Birch 2025). [trial evidence] |
| Stabilizer | Gluteus medius, adductors | Keep knees tracking over the toes and the feet together on the side hop. [clinical opinion] |
| Stabilizer | Core (abdominals, spinal extensors) | Keep the trunk tall and quiet so the hop comes from the legs; GymVisual highlights core. [clinical opinion] |

### Landing norms

| Measure | Value from the literature | Task it comes from | Use for the animation | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hop rhythm | Preferred rate about 2.2 hops per second, the same in place and hopping forward [trial evidence] | Two-leg hopping in place and forward | About 0.45 s per hop cycle | farley1991, birch2025 |
| Ground contact time | 236 to 260 ms at 2.2 Hz; about 191 ms at 3.0 Hz [trial evidence] | Two-leg hopping in place, young and older adults | Contact about 0.20 to 0.26 s; air time about 0.2 s | hobara2015 |

| Ground contact time | 160 to 200 ms; faster pace shortened it, hop distance (30 vs 45 cm) did not [trial evidence] | Single-leg lateral hops | Side hop can stay as quick as the others | suwanmana2026 |
| Ground contact time | About 186 ms at maximal effort [trial evidence] | Two-leg repeated maximal hopping | Upper bound for a fast, springy version | kovacs2021 |

| Ankle | Sagittal range about 29 to 34 degrees; side-to-side range 7.5 to 14 degrees, larger at 45 cm than 30 cm [trial evidence] | Single-leg lateral hops | Keep side hops short so the ankle does not roll | suwanmana2026 |
| Knee flexion | Knee flexion amplitude about 0.34 rad (about 20 degrees) during repeated hopping [trial evidence] | Two-leg maximal hopping | Knee bends roughly 15 to 25 degrees from contact to lowest point (our estimate, clinical opinion) | kovacs2021 |
| Knee flexion at contact | About 9 to 13 degrees, rising from forward to lateral landings [trial evidence, pilot of 5 athletes] | Single-leg step-off landings in four directions | Knees soft, not locked, at touchdown, about 10 degrees | sinsurin2015 |
| Hip flexion | No data found for this kind of hopping. Evidence gap | n/a | Small hip bend that follows the knee; trunk stays tall (clinical opinion) | none |
| Hop distance | No study of this drill. Hexagon test: hop from the center over a 60 cm side and back [trial evidence for the test layout] | Two-foot hexagon agility test | About 20 to 40 cm out from the center per hop (clinical opinion) | topend-hexagon, beekhuizen2009 |

Note on low hops: when people hopped lower at a fixed rate, contact time got longer (218 ms at max height vs 386 ms at the lowest) and joints flexed more at the bottom (Bobbert 2011). So "small hop" does not automatically mean "stiff, short contact". The drill can be done springy (short contacts, mostly ankle) or soft (longer contacts, more knee). [trial evidence]

### Breathing

No study covers breathing for this drill. Breathe steadily through the set and do not hold the breath; a short, light exhale on each rebound or every few hops helps keep the rhythm. [clinical opinion]

### Prescription

- Count foot contacts. Beginners: 80 to 100 foot contacts per session in total across all jumping drills (Davies 2015, Table 4); intermediate 100 to 120. [clinical opinion, peer-reviewed commentary]
- Practical dose: 3 to 4 sets of 10 to 20 s (or 4 to 6 rounds of the three-point pattern), 30 to 60 s rest between sets. [clinical opinion]
- Frequency: 2 to 3 sessions per week; 48 to 72 h between high-intensity plyometric sessions (Davies 2015; Physiopedia). As a low-intensity warm-up it can be done more often. [clinical opinion]
- Progression: bilateral before single-leg, stable before unstable, simple before complex (Physiopedia). Then go faster, add distance, or move to a single-leg version. [clinical opinion]
- Include a practice round: on the hexagon test, times improved between a first trial and a same-day retest, a learning effect (Beekhuizen 2009). [trial evidence]

### Cues

1. Stand tall, feet hip-width, arms bent and held in front of the chest.
2. Hop with both feet together to the first point, then back to the center.
3. Land softly on the balls of the feet, knees slightly bent, then bounce straight out again.
4. Keep the hops small and quick; think "light and quiet".
5. Face forward the whole time; only the feet change direction.
6. Knees stay over the toes on every landing, including the side hop.
7. Keep the trunk tall and the arms quiet; breathe steadily.

All cues [clinical opinion]; cues 1 to 3 follow the GymVisual movement description.

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Landing flat-footed or heels first with a loud thud | Land on the balls of the feet and let the heels lower slightly; aim for quiet contacts. [clinical opinion] |
| Stiff, locked knees at touchdown | Keep a small knee bend at contact (about 10 degrees was typical in multidirectional landings, Sinsurin 2015). [trial evidence for the angle; fix is clinical opinion] |
| Knees caving inward, especially on the side hop | Push the knees out over the toes; shorten the side hop until it is controlled. [clinical opinion] |
| Hops too big or too far, so each contact becomes a sink and reset | Shrink the hop; the drill is about rhythm. Wider lateral hops increased side-to-side ankle motion (Suwanmana 2026). [trial evidence, single-leg] |
| Turning the hips or shoulders to face the direction of travel | Face forward the whole time, as in the hexagon test. [clinical opinion] |
| Feet splitting apart or landing one foot at a time | Keep feet together on take-off and landing. [clinical opinion] |
| Leaning the trunk forward or flailing the arms | Chest up, arms bent in front, core lightly braced. [clinical opinion] |

### Safety and who should modify

- Prerequisites before jumping drills: a base of strength, and single-leg balance of about 30 s with eyes open and closed (Davies 2015). The authors call missing foundational strength the most significant contraindication. [clinical opinion, peer-reviewed commentary]
- Do not hop with pain, acute inflammation, an acute or subacute sprain or strain, joint instability, or soon after surgery unless a clinician has cleared it (Davies 2015; Physiopedia). [clinical opinion]
- Take care with a history of stress fractures or other stress-related injury, and avoid unyielding surfaces (Physiopedia). Use a firm, non-slip surface and supportive shoes. [clinical opinion]
- Recent ankle sprain or ankle instability: start with forward/back only and add the side hop later. Lateral landings showed poorer early knee coordination than forward or diagonal ones (Sinsurin 2020). [trial evidence for the landing finding; the advice is clinical opinion]
- Older adults: two-leg hopping at 2.2 to 3.0 Hz was feasible in older adults, with leg stiffness similar to young adults (Hobara 2015); still, start smaller and slower. [trial evidence for feasibility; advice is clinical opinion]
- Lower-impact option: replace hops with quick steps to the three points, or do the pattern as small heel-raise bounces in place. [clinical opinion]
- Pregnancy, pelvic floor symptoms, osteoporosis or significant joint disease: no specific evidence found; ask a clinician first. [clinical opinion]

## 1. Scientific studies

### Hopping mechanics (contact time, rhythm, joint motion)

- [Farley et al. 1991, Hopping frequency in humans (J Appl Physiol, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1778902/). Preferred hop rate about 2.2 per second, in place and forward.
- [Birch, Kelly and Farris 2025, Surface stiffness and preferred hopping frequency (Scand J Med Sci Sports, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12093081/). Two-leg hopping: contact 257 to 268 ms at 2.2 Hz; ankle angles; soleus and foot intrinsic EMG.
- [Hobara et al. 2015, Leg stiffness of older and younger adults over a range of hopping frequencies (J Electromyogr Kinesiol, PDF)](https://staff.aist.go.jp/e.yoshida/papers/JEK2015Hobara.pdf). Two-leg hopping in place: contact about 0.19 to 0.26 s by frequency.
- [Jessup et al. 2025, Frequency and leg stiffness adaptation in vertical hopping with added load (J Exp Biol, eScholarship PDF)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8c30v9b1/qt8c30v9b1.pdf). Two-leg hopping: leg stiffness changes driven mainly by the ankle, not the knee.
- [Kovacs, Sebestyen and Tihanyi 2021, Lower leg characteristics and hopping height (Acta Polytechnica Hungarica, PDF)](https://acta.uni-obuda.hu/Kovacs_Sebestyen_Tihanyi_112.pdf). Repeated two-leg hopping: contact 0.186 s; knee flexion amplitude 0.34 rad, ankle 0.51 rad.
- [Bobbert and Casius 2011, Spring-like leg behaviour in maximum and submaximum hopping (Phil Trans R Soc B, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3130449). Lower hops at a fixed rate gave longer contacts and lower leg stiffness.
- [Suwanmana et al. 2026, Ankle biomechanics and muscle activation in repeated single-leg lateral hopping (Biomechanics, PDF)](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/29190/1/Ankle%20Biomechanics%20and%20Muscle%20Activation%20During%20Repeated%20Single%20Leg%20Lateral%20Hopping%20Effects%20of%20Speed%20and%20Distance.pdf). 30 and 45 cm lateral hops: contact 160 to 200 ms; soleus, peroneus and tibialis anterior EMG. Small sample (12).

### Multidirectional landings and hop tests

- [Sinsurin et al. 2015, Lower extremity flexion angles landing in multiple directions (ISBS proceedings)](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/6632/5991). Knee flexion at contact about 9 to 13 degrees, forward to lateral. Pilot study, 5 athletes, single-leg step-off landings.
- [Sinsurin et al. 2020, Knee joint coordination during single-leg landing in different directions (Sports Biomechanics, repository)](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/24497/). Lateral landings showed poorer early knee coordination than forward and diagonal.
- [Beekhuizen et al. 2009, Test-retest reliability and minimal detectable change of the hexagon agility test (J Strength Cond Res, abstract)](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/hpd_pt_facarticles/89). Two-foot multidirectional hop test; ICC 0.92 to 0.94; MDC 1.015 s; practice trial advised.
- [Hernandez-Davo et al. 2021, Sprint, change of direction, jump and hexagon test in young tennis players (J Sports Sci Med)](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-20-197.xml-Fulltext). Hexagon test with 60 cm sides, feet together; ICC 0.88.
- [Negrete et al. 2021, Test-retest reliability of the T-Drill hop test (IJSPT, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8169018). Single-leg forward, lateral and backward hops; excellent reliability; validity not yet established.
- [Sawle, Freeman and Marsden 2017, Intra-rater reliability of the multiple single-leg hop-stabilization test (IJSPT, abstract)](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/99588). Multidirectional hop-and-stick balance test; ICC 0.85.
- [Quadrant hop test reliability and validity, 2024 (Cureus, abstract index)](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-38883057). Multidirectional hop test in 119 amateur athletes; high rater agreement. Protocol details not in the abstract.

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

- [Davies, Riemann and Manske 2015, Current concepts of plyometric exercise (IJSPT clinical commentary, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4637913). Progression, 80 to 100 beginner foot contacts, 48 to 72 h rest, prerequisites and contraindications.
- [Physiopedia, Plyometrics](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Plyometrics). Stretch-shortening cycle phases, progression order, contraindications, 2 to 3 sessions per week.
- [Physiopedia, Hop Test](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Hop_Test). Single, triple, crossover and timed hop tests; under 10% side-to-side difference target.
- [Topend Sports, Hexagon agility test](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/hexagon.htm). Two-foot, face-forward hexagon protocol; closest tested layout to this drill.
