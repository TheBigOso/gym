# 3 Sec Sumo Squat: research

Batch 1, exercise 9 of 10 (GitHub issue #43). Movement reference: GymVisual #735012, described in `reference.md` in this folder. Researched 2026-11-26.

**Tags.** [trial evidence] = backed by a measured study or a meta-analysis of studies. [clinical opinion] = coaching or clinical guidance, or our own reasoning, with no direct trial behind it. Most stance-width studies used **loaded barbell back squats in trained lifters**, not bodyweight squats. Applying them to a bodyweight sumo squat is an extrapolation, and the card says so where it matters.

### Muscles

| Role | Muscles | Notes and tag |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Quadriceps (vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, rectus femoris) | Main knee extensors in any squat. Stance width did not change quadriceps EMG in two EMG studies (McCaw 1999; Paoli 2009). Larsen 2021 found more vastus lateralis activity with a narrow stance, and Sinclair 2022 modelled lower quadriceps force with a wide stance. So the sumo stance keeps the quadriceps working but probably does not raise their demand. [trial evidence] |
| Target | Gluteus maximus | Wider stances raised gluteus maximus EMG, including in the unloaded (0% 1RM) condition (Paoli 2009), and raised modelled gluteal force (Sinclair 2022, Sports). [trial evidence] |
| Target | Adductors (adductor magnus, adductor longus) | Evidence is **mixed**. McCaw 1999 reported adductor longus activity changing with stance width (larger in the wide stance, as summarised by Sinclair 2022, Sensors). Paoli 2009 found no width effect on adductor magnus, and Larsen 2021 found none on adductor longus. Keep the adductors highlighted to match the GymVisual reference, but do not claim the sumo stance "isolates" them. [trial evidence, mixed] |
| Synergist | Hamstrings (semitendinosus, biceps femoris) | Assist hip extension; no stance-width effect in Paoli 2009. [trial evidence] |
| Synergist | Soleus and gastrocnemius | Control the shin. Larsen 2021 found more calf activity with a narrow stance, so the wide stance asks less of them. [trial evidence] |
| Stabilizer | Gluteus medius and minimus | Hold the knees out over the toes. No stance-width effect on gluteus medius EMG (Paoli 2009; Larsen 2021). [trial evidence for the EMG; role is clinical opinion] |
| Stabilizer | Erector spinae and deep core | Keep the trunk upright, especially during the slow lowering. [clinical opinion] |
| Stabilizer | Hip external rotators, tibialis anterior, foot intrinsics | Keep the turned-out foot and knee aligned and the arch from collapsing. [clinical opinion] |

### Angle norms

| Measure | Start | Bottom | Norm | Sources | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stance width | About 1.5x shoulder width (reference) | Unchanged | Studies' "wide" stances: 140% of shoulder width (McCaw 1999); 1.7x acromion width, mean about 60 cm (Larsen 2021); 150-200% of greater-trochanter distance (Paoli 2009); 1.5x trochanter width (Sinclair 2022, Sports); 2x the distance between the front hip bones, about 0.48 m (Lorenzetti 2018). 1.4-1.7x shoulder width matches the reference. | mccaw1999, larsen2021, paoli2009, sinclair2022sports, lorenzetti2018 | [trial evidence] for the widths tested; no study sets an "optimal" sumo width |
| Toe-out (foot angle) | About 30-45° per foot (reference) | Unchanged | Angles tested: 0°, 21° and 42° (Lorenzetti 2018; Sinclair 2022, Sensors). Lorenzetti flagged **wide stance with 0° toe-out** as an extreme position to avoid and suggested wide stance at about 21° for knee control. Self-selected toe-out in barbell squats averaged about 20° (Sinclair 2022, Sensors). Use about 30° for the animation: within the tested range. | lorenzetti2018, sinclair2022sensors | [trial evidence] for 0-42°; the exact 30-45° sumo value is [clinical opinion] |
| Hip abduction | Set by stance and toe-out | Increases as the hips sink | More toe-out and a wider stance both increase hip abduction. Peak about 33° at 42° toe-out vs about 20° at 0° (Sinclair 2022, Sensors, habitual stance width). | sinclair2022sensors, larsen2021 | [trial evidence] |
| Knee flexion | About 0-5° | About 100-120° at thighs-parallel | Wide stance at the bottom of loaded 3RM back squats: about 119-120° (Larsen 2021). Wide stances reduced peak knee flexion by 5-7° vs narrow (Larsen 2021) and at mid-lift (Sinclair 2022, Sports). Our thighs-parallel bodyweight target sits at or slightly above this. | larsen2021, sinclair2022sports | [trial evidence] for loaded squats; bodyweight value is [clinical opinion] |
| Hip flexion | About 0° | About 100-112° | About 110-112° at the bottom across stance and bar conditions (Larsen 2021); wider stances and larger foot angles increase hip and knee range of motion (Lorenzetti 2018). | larsen2021, lorenzetti2018 | [trial evidence] for loaded squats |
| Trunk angle (forward lean from vertical) | Upright, 0-5° | About 20-35° (animation suggestion) | Loaded high-bar wide squats showed a torso angle of about 46° at the bottom (low-bar: about 53°) (Larsen 2021). Hands clasped at the chest with no bar should allow a more upright trunk; no study measured this for the bodyweight sumo squat. | larsen2021 | Loaded value [trial evidence]; bodyweight target [clinical opinion] |
| Knee line (front view) | Knee over 2nd-3rd toe | Knee over 2nd-3rd toe | Wider stances reduced side-to-side knee drift; larger foot angles increased it. Novices drifted more than experienced squatters (Lorenzetti 2018). ACE cues "knees over the second toe." | lorenzetti2018, ace_bwsquat | [trial evidence] + [clinical opinion] |

### Breathing

- Inhale slowly through the 3-second lowering, brace lightly at the bottom pause, exhale as you stand. ACE gives the same inhale-down, exhale-up pattern for the bodyweight squat. [clinical opinion]
- Cadence for the 8-second loop: 3 s down (inhale), about 1 s pause (brace), about 1-1.5 s up (exhale), then a short reset standing (natural breath). [clinical opinion]
- Bodyweight load does not call for a full Valsalva hold. [clinical opinion]

### Prescription

| Field | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Sets | 2-4 | [clinical opinion] |
| Reps | 8-15 | [clinical opinion] |
| Tempo | 3-1-1-0 (3 s down, 1 s pause, about 1 s up, no pause at the top). Total rep about 5 s, inside the 2-8 s range that Korakakis 2023 drew from the meta-analytic data. | [trial evidence] for the rep-duration range; the exact split is [clinical opinion] |
| Time under tension | 10 reps x 5 s = about 50 s per set, within the 20-70 s per set that Wilk 2021 proposed for hypertrophy (the review says no data establish optimal values). | [clinical opinion, from a review] |
| Frequency | 2-3 times a week, or as a warm-up before lower-body training | [clinical opinion] |
| Progression | Lengthen the pause, add reps, then hold a dumbbell or kettlebell at the chest (goblet) or between the legs | [clinical opinion] |

**What the 3-second lowering does and does not do:**

- Pooled across 9 studies (166 people), eccentric phases over 2 s did not change hypertrophy compared with phases of 2 s or less. In trained people they gave similar or slightly larger strength gains, but shorter eccentrics were better for jump height (Amdi and King 2025). [trial evidence]
- In parallel squats, 2 s vs 4 s eccentrics produced no difference in thigh cross-sectional area (Shibata 2021, as summarised by Korakakis 2023). [trial evidence]
- Wilk 2021 concluded the best tempo is unclear but favours slow lowering paired with a fast rise; a 4-s eccentric also felt harder (higher RPE) than 2-s or self-paced reps in one study they cite. [trial evidence, review level]
- Evidence is thin for the bodyweight case: none of these tempo studies used a bodyweight sumo squat, and none tested a bottom pause. The slow lowering is best justified as a **control and technique tool** that makes a light load harder. [clinical opinion]

### Cues

1. **Set up:** Feet about 1.5x shoulder width, toes turned out about 30°, hands clasped at the chest. [clinical opinion; stance and toe-out within tested ranges, trial evidence]
2. **Brace:** Ribs down, chest tall, light core brace before you move. [clinical opinion]
3. **Down (count 3):** Breathe in and sit the hips straight down between the heels: "one, two, three." [clinical opinion]
4. **Knees:** Push the knees out over the toes the whole way down. [clinical opinion, supported by knee-drift data in Lorenzetti 2018]
5. **Bottom:** Pause for a beat with thighs about parallel, trunk upright, heels flat. [clinical opinion]
6. **Up:** Breathe out and drive through the whole foot, squeezing the glutes to stand tall. [clinical opinion]
7. **Reset:** Stand fully, re-brace, repeat. [clinical opinion]

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Rushing the lowering (dropping in 1 s) | Count "one, two, three" out loud; the descent is the point of the drill. | [clinical opinion] |
| Knees caving inside the toes | Push the knees out over the 2nd-3rd toe; reduce depth until you can hold alignment. Novices show more knee drift than experienced squatters. | [trial evidence] for drift; fix is [clinical opinion] |
| Toes pointing straight ahead in a wide stance | Turn the toes out to match the knees; wide stance at 0° was flagged as an extreme position by Lorenzetti 2018. | [trial evidence] |
| Folding forward or hinging like a deadlift | Keep the chest tall and the hands at the chest; sit down between the heels, not back. | [clinical opinion] |
| Heels or inner arches lifting | Keep weight across the whole foot; narrow the stance slightly if the heels rise. ACE: stop descending if the heels lift. | [clinical opinion] |
| Stance so wide the feet slide or the groin pulls | Bring the feet in to about 1.4x shoulder width. Wide stances push more force sideways into the floor (Sinclair 2022, Sports), so use grippy footwear or a non-slip floor. | [trial evidence] for sideways force; fix is [clinical opinion] |
| Bouncing out of the bottom | Hold the pause for a full beat before standing. | [clinical opinion] |

### Safety and who should modify

- **Groin or adductor strain history:** Use a narrower stance and less depth; build up over weeks. [clinical opinion]
- **Hip pain, including hip impingement symptoms or after hip surgery:** Wide stance changes hip position (Larsen 2021 measured more hip abduction and internal rotation with a wide stance). Reduce stance width and depth, or follow the clinician's limits. [trial evidence for the hip position; advice is clinical opinion]
- **Knee pain:** Squat only to a pain-free depth and keep the knee over the toes. [clinical opinion]
- **Poor balance or older adults:** Hold a chair or rail, or squat to a box set at a comfortable height. [clinical opinion]
- **Pregnancy and postpartum:** Follow clinician guidance; wide-stance squats are often kept but depth and breath-holding may need limits. No trial was found for this group. [clinical opinion]
- **Stop if** sharp pain, numbness or giving-way occurs. [clinical opinion]

### Evidence gaps (stated plainly)

- No peer-reviewed study was found that tested the **bodyweight** sumo squat itself, its ideal width, or a 30-45° toe-out.
- Adductor findings conflict between studies.
- Tempo studies show a slow eccentric is not harmful and may help strength in trained people, but no tempo study matches this exact drill.
- Sumo-specific pages from NASM, NSCA and Physiopedia could not be retrieved in this session, so section 2 relies on ACE and one coaching-science abstract summary.

## 1. Scientific studies

### Stance width and muscle activity

- [Paoli, Marcolin and Petrone 2009, effect of stance width on EMG of eight thigh muscles during the back squat (J Strength Cond Res, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19130646/). Six lifters, stance at 100/150/200% of trochanter distance, 0-70% 1RM: only gluteus maximus rose with width; adductor magnus and quadriceps did not change.
- [Same study, full text (University of Padova repository)](https://research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/2461435/1/the%20effect%20of%20stance%20width%202009.pdf)
- [McCaw and Melrose 1999, stance width and bar load effects on leg muscle activity in the parallel squat (Med Sci Sports Exerc, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10188748/). Nine men at 75/100/140% of shoulder width: width affected gluteus maximus and adductor longus but not the quadriceps.
- [Larsen et al. 2021, stance width and bar placement in back squats: kinematics, kinetics and EMG (Front Sports Act Living, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8440835). 18 lifters, 0.7x vs 1.7x acromion width: wide stance gave less knee flexion and more hip abduction; adductor longus unchanged.
- [Sinclair et al. 2022, multi-experiment study of stance width in the barbell squat (Sports, repository PDF)](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/43838/3/sports-10-00136-v2.pdf). Modelled forces in 70 + 20 men: wide stance raised gluteal force and lowered quadriceps force; adductors not modelled.
- [Krause Neto et al. 2020, gluteus maximus activation during common exercises: systematic review (J Sports Sci Med, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7039033). Squat variants ranged widely in gluteal activation depending on bar, stance and depth.

### Foot angle, knee alignment and joint angles

- [Lorenzetti et al. 2018, how to squat: stance widths, foot angles and experience (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6050697). 42 adults, 3 widths x 0/21/42°: avoid wide stance with 0° toe-out; wider stance reduced knee drift.
- [Sinclair et al. 2022, habitual and manipulated foot angles in the barbell back squat (Sensors, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9501107). Self-selected toe-out about 20°; 42° gave the most hip abduction; no single best foot angle.

### Eccentric tempo and repetition duration

- [Amdi and King 2025, effect of eccentric phase duration on strength, hypertrophy and jump height: systematic review and meta-analysis (J Sports Sci, AUT repository)](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/bitstreams/897681c9-8d6d-4595-b739-3ab5b25ca8b1/download). 9 studies: eccentrics over 2 s matched hypertrophy, possibly more strength in trained people, less jump gain.
- [Wilk, Zajac and Tufano 2021, movement tempo and strength and hypertrophy: a review (Sports Med, Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01465-2). Best tempo unclear; favours slow eccentric with fast concentric; proposes 20-70 s per set for hypertrophy without firm data.
- [Korakakis et al. 2023, optimizing resistance training technique for hypertrophy: narrative review (J Funct Morphol Kinesiol, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801605/). Recommends 2-8 s per rep (drawing on Schoenfeld 2015); summarises Shibata 2021 (2 s vs 4 s squat eccentric, no difference).

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

- [ACE Exercise Library: Bodyweight Squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/). Knees over the second toe, inhale down and exhale up, stop if heels lift or the back rounds (clinical opinion).
- [ACE Exercise Library: Back Squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/11/back-squat/). Stance slightly wider than shoulder width, chest up (clinical opinion; loaded version).
- [Coaching Science Abstracts (SDSU): summary of McCaw and Melrose 1999](https://coachsci.sdsu.edu/csa/vol73/mccaw.htm). Plain-language summary: stance width changed inner-thigh and buttock activity, not the quadriceps.
