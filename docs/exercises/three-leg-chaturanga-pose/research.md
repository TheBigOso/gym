# 3 Leg Chaturanga Pose: research

Batch 1, exercise 6 of 10 (GitHub issue #28). GymVisual reference #591513. Researched 2026-11-26.

The flow: high plank, push back to downward-facing dog (adho mukha svanasana), lift one leg into three-legged dog (eka pada adho mukha svanasana), then shift forward and lower into chaturanga dandasana (four-limbed staff pose, low plank) with that leg still lifted. Return to plank and switch legs.

**How to read the tags.** [trial evidence] means a measured result from a study (often small and lab-based, rarely a true trial). [clinical opinion] means a teacher's, clinician's or author's view without study data behind it.

**Where the evidence is thin, plainly:**

- No study found measures the 3 leg chaturanga flow or three-legged dog as such. Everything here is built from studies of the parts (plank, low plank, downward dog, Surya Namaskar poses).
- No study found reports triceps, pectoralis or shoulder joint loads in chaturanga itself. The triceps/chest/front-shoulder roles below are inferred from the push-up-like shape [clinical opinion].
- Most angle and EMG data come from small samples (8 to 30 people), usually trained yoga practitioners.
- The research budget ran out before searching push-up EMG studies, yoga injury surveys, or force-plate data on hand loading in downward dog. Yoga Journal pose pages, the Polis 2015 pregnancy paper, and ACE pages would not load; they are not cited.

### Muscles

Suggested rig IDs in brackets (from `src/rig.json`).

**Target**

- Triceps [triceps]: control the lowering into chaturanga and hold the elbows near 90 degrees [clinical opinion; no chaturanga triceps EMG found].
- Pectoralis major and anterior deltoid [pec, ant_deltoid]: lower and hold the body, as at the bottom of a push-up. Crandell says mini push-ups train "your chest, your arms, and the front of your shoulders" [clinical opinion].
- Glute max and hamstrings of the lifted leg [glute_max, hamstrings]: hold the leg in hip extension in three-legged dog and through the lowering [clinical opinion; no EMG of three-legged dog found]. Kelley 2018 found biceps femoris and glute med activity low in regular two-legged downward dog [trial evidence], so the leg lift is what adds work here.

**Synergist**

- Serratus anterior [serratus]: holds the shoulder blades on the rib cage in dog and chaturanga. In a 2024 EMG abstract, experts used more stabilising muscles such as serratus anterior in downward dog and chaturanga, while intermediates over-relied on anterior deltoid and upper trapezius [trial evidence, n=20, conference abstract].
- Latissimus dorsi [lats]: Crandell says strong lats are "essential for a healthy Chaturanga" [clinical opinion]. Mullerpatan 2020 measured about 35% MVC lats in Ashtangasana and Parvatasana (Surya Namaskar poses close to chaturanga and dog) [trial evidence, n=8].
- External oblique and rectus abdominis [obliques, core]: Ni 2014 found high plank, low plank and downward dog effective for external oblique work [trial evidence, n=30]. Salem 2013 reported appreciable rectus abdominis activity in all poses studied, including wall-supported downward dog [trial evidence, older adults].

**Stabilizer**

- Rotator cuff and posterior shoulder [infraspinatus, post_deltoid]: keep the humeral head centred as the elbows bend [clinical opinion; Crandell's surgeon source notes the shoulder relies on muscles and ligaments for stability].
- Glute med of the standing leg and both sides [glute_med]: keep the pelvis square when one leg is up [clinical opinion].
- Quadriceps [quads]: keep the standing knee straight in plank and chaturanga [clinical opinion].
- Spinal extensors [spinal_extensors]: keep a long, neutral spine in plank and chaturanga [clinical opinion].
- Wrist and forearm flexors/extensors (no rig ID): manage weight through the hands [clinical opinion].

**Lengthened**

- Hamstrings and calves of the standing leg [hamstrings, calves] in downward dog [clinical opinion; the Wikipedia pose summary says the pose stretches hamstrings and calves].

### Angle norms

| Measure | Norm | Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elbow, chaturanga | About 90 degrees ("bent at a right angle"), elbows close to the ribs | [clinical opinion] | Wikipedia Chaturanga; Crandell Part III (block option with "elbows bent 90 degrees") |
| Elbow, deeper lowering | Eight-limb pose (chest to the floor, deeper than chaturanga) measured 116.3 ± 17.0 degrees elbow flexion; so dropping below chaturanga takes the elbow well past 90 | [trial evidence, n=10] | Mullerpatan 2019 |
| Shoulder, chaturanga | Upper arm roughly parallel to the floor; "keep your torso level with your elbows". Going lower moves the shoulder into extension | [clinical opinion] | Crandell Part I |
| Shoulder, eight-limb pose | Reported as -25.8 ± 12.4 degrees; the paper does not define the shoulder sign convention, so treat as "some extension" only | [trial evidence, n=10, unclear convention] | Mullerpatan 2019 |
| Wrist extension | Downward dog about 50 degrees; hand balances (and by extension chaturanga) about 90 degrees | [clinical opinion] | Yoga International (Spindler) |
| Wrist extension, measured | Eight-limb pose 85.4 ± 13.4 degrees, the highest wrist extension in the Surya Namaskar sequence | [trial evidence, n=10] | Mullerpatan 2019 |
| Wrist position, plank | Hyperextended wrist gave larger wrist flexion angles and ground reaction force than a neutral wrist; authors suggest a neutral wrist | [trial evidence, n=9, conference paper] | Hawke 2020 |
| Hip, downward dog | Inverted V with hips as high as possible. The rig target should be "trunk and thighs close to a right angle or tighter", limited by hamstring length; this number is the author's estimate, not from a source | [clinical opinion; thin] | Wikipedia Downward Dog |
| Hip, Parvatasana (Surya Namaskar mountain pose) | Mullerpatan 2019 tabulated 49 to 67 degrees hip flexion, but the table was partly garbled and the convention is relative; do not use as a hard norm | [trial evidence, unreliable for this use] | Mullerpatan 2019 |
| Lifted leg, three-legged dog | Leg roughly in line with the trunk, hips kept level (not opened). No source verified | [clinical opinion; no source] | none |

### Breathing

- In Ashtanga practice chaturanga is done on an exhalation (Wikipedia Chaturanga) [clinical opinion].
- Suggested pattern: exhale as the hips push back to downward dog; take 2 to 3 breaths there; inhale as the leg lifts to three-legged dog; exhale as you shift forward and lower to chaturanga; inhale back to plank [clinical opinion; no trial data].
- Keep breathing in any hold; do not hold the breath while lowering [clinical opinion].

### Prescription

No study tests sets, reps or holds for this flow. All of the following is [clinical opinion].

- Downward dog: 2 to 5 breaths before the leg lift.
- Three-legged dog: 2 to 3 breaths with the leg up.
- Lowering to chaturanga: about 2 to 3 s, controlled, then either touch-and-go or hold 1 breath. Iyengar-style practice holds chaturanga for set times such as 30 s with continuous breathing (Wikipedia Chaturanga), which is an advanced option.
- Reps: 3 to 5 per side, alternating legs, 1 to 3 rounds. Stop the set when the shoulders start dropping below the elbows.
- Frequency: as part of a flow or warm-up, 2 to 4 days a week.

### Cues

1. Start in high plank, hands under the shoulders, body in one line, fingers spread [clinical opinion].
2. Press the floor away and lift the hips up and back to downward dog; bend the knees if the back rounds. YogaUOnline: bending the knees "preserves the length of the spine" [clinical opinion].
3. Root through the base of the index finger and thumb, not the heel of the hand (Keller; YogaUOnline) [clinical opinion].
4. Lift one leg behind you in line with the trunk; keep both hip points facing the floor [clinical opinion].
5. Keep the leg up, shift forward until the shoulders are ahead of the wrists, then bend the elbows straight back [clinical opinion].
6. "Hug your elbows into your side-body" (Crandell Part I) [clinical opinion].
7. Stop when the shoulders reach elbow height; "keep your torso level with your elbows" (Crandell Part I) [clinical opinion].

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Dropping the chest below the elbows | Stop at elbow height; use a block under the sternum or a strap above the elbows to learn the depth (Crandell Parts I and III). Crandell's source says the error reduces shoulder stability and can lead to impingement or rotator cuff strain | [clinical opinion] |
| Elbows flaring out to the sides | Hug the elbows to the ribs, pointing straight back (Crandell Part I; Wikipedia) | [clinical opinion] |
| Weight pooling in the heel of the hand, hand "buckling" in transitions | Press through the finger mounds, especially the index; spread the fingers; consider a wedge (Keller; Spindler; YogaUOnline) | [clinical opinion] |
| Shrugging and over-using the upper traps and front shoulders | Press the floor away and widen the shoulder blades. Experts in a 2024 EMG abstract used less anterior deltoid and upper trapezius than intermediates in dog and chaturanga | [trial evidence, conference abstract] for the muscle pattern; [clinical opinion] for the cue |
| Hips sagging or piking in plank and chaturanga | Brace the trunk so the body stays in one line; planks engage the external oblique (Ni 2014) | [trial evidence] for the muscle; [clinical opinion] for the cue |
| Lifted leg swings up and the hip opens, pelvis twists | Lift only as high as the hips stay level and square to the floor | [clinical opinion; no source verified] |
| Lowering without shifting forward first | Move the shoulders ahead of the wrists before the elbows bend, so the hands end low, near the lower ribs; Wikipedia says the hands sit "just above the pelvis" | [clinical opinion] |

### Safety and who should modify

- **Wrists.** Chaturanga and plank need near-maximal wrist extension; Mullerpatan measured about 85 degrees in the eight-limb pose [trial evidence]. People with wrist pain, numbness or night tingling (carpal tunnel signs) or arthritis should modify (Spindler) [clinical opinion]. Options: wedge or folded blanket under the heel of the hand, forearm plank, dolphin instead of downward dog (Spindler; Keller) [clinical opinion]. Keller adds that persistent wrist pain can come from the shoulder, so see a doctor [clinical opinion]. A neutral wrist lowered ground reaction force in side plank in one small lab study (Hawke 2020) [trial evidence].
- **Shoulders.** Avoid shoulder extension and elbow flare in chaturanga (Crandell, citing an orthopedic shoulder surgeon) [clinical opinion]. Modify with knees down, a block under the sternum, or a strap above the elbows; with both shoulder and wrist problems, replace chaturanga with locust (Crandell Part III) [clinical opinion]. A chair or strap-supported downward dog reduces load on the shoulders (Wikipedia Downward Dog; Shelley) [clinical opinion].
- **Pregnancy.** The Wikipedia Downward Dog article reports a 2015 study (Polis et al.) of 26 poses, including downward dog, with no ill effects in healthy women at 35 to 37 weeks [trial evidence, primary paper not read]. No data were found on chaturanga or the leg-lifted version in pregnancy. Suggest knees-down or skipping chaturanga in later pregnancy, and checking with a prenatal provider [clinical opinion].
- **Beginners and older adults.** Wall-supported downward dog was used as the introductory version in a seniors study (Salem 2013) [trial evidence for its use]. Learn plank, knees-down chaturanga and regular downward dog before adding the leg lift [clinical opinion].
- **Jumping back.** A Yoga Journal (May 2018) piece links the jump-back to chaturanga with recent shoulder and wrist injuries; this flow lowers from dog rather than jumping, which avoids that landing [clinical opinion].

## 1. Scientific studies

- [Mullerpatan et al. 2019, Kinematics of Suryanamaskar using 3D motion capture (Int J Yoga, PMC6521759, PDF)](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/5454d7121b1d4dc732973d81227041c7/PMC6521759.pdf). 10 trained practitioners; eight-limb pose had 116 degrees elbow flexion and 85 degrees wrist extension.
- [Mullerpatan et al. 2020, Muscle activity using surface EMG while performing Surya Namaskar (Int J Yoga, PubMed 32669768)](https://www.pubmed.gov/32669768/). 8 practitioners; lats about 35% MVC in Ashtangasana and Parvatasana; no arm muscles recorded.
- [Ni et al. 2014, Core muscle function during specific yoga poses (Complement Ther Med)](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Core-muscle-function-during-specific-yoga/991031576452002976). 30 practitioners; high plank, low plank and downward dog work the external oblique.
- [Williams, Dimitrov and Makin 2024, Motor control in yoga, EMG in experts vs intermediates (Proc Physiol Soc, abstract)](https://www.physoc.org/?p=118916). In dog and chaturanga, experts used less anterior deltoid/upper trap and more serratus; n=20, abstract only.
- [Hawke et al. 2020, Joint angles and forces on wrist structures during yoga pose variations (ISBS conference, PDF)](https://core.ac.uk/download/327694081.pdf). n=9; hyperextended vs neutral wrist in plank, side plank and upward dog; recommends a neutral wrist.
- [Salem et al. 2013, Physical demand profiles of hatha yoga postures performed by older adults (Evid Based Complement Alternat Med)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3814077). Wall-supported downward dog in seniors: hip extensor moment 0.56 Nm/kg, hamstrings about 50% of walking EMG; lower limbs only. Content read from a full-text copy because the PMC page blocked automated reading.
- [Kelley et al. 2018, EMG analysis of selected asana in experienced practitioners (J Bodyw Mov Ther)](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1989584713). n=13; in downward dog tibialis anterior was most active and hamstring/glute activity was low.
- [Chopp-Hurley et al. 2018, Scapular muscle activity during static yoga postures (JOSPT)](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/display/publication1534806). 20 women, 15 poses, trapezius and serratus 3 to 57% MVIC; the abstract does not say whether dog or chaturanga were included.

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

- [Jason Crandell, The expert's guide to chaturanga, Part I (with orthopedic shoulder surgeon Paul Roache, MD)](https://jasonyoga.com/2016/04/29/the-experts-guide-to-practicing-and-teaching-chaturanga). Elbows hugged in, torso no lower than the elbows; explains impingement and rotator cuff risk.
- [Jason Crandell, Part II: 5 poses to make your chaturanga strong and steady](https://www.jasonyoga.com/2016/05/17/5-poses-to-make-your-chaturanga-strong-and-steady/). Prep work: forearm plank, mini push-ups, lat work, locust, low cobra.
- [Jason Crandell, Part III: chaturanga modifications](https://www.jasonyoga.com/2016/05/26/chaturanga-modifications/). Bolster, block at 90 degree elbows, elbow strap, knees down, locust for shoulder and wrist issues.
- [Yoga International, Beth Spindler: Wrist support when weight-bearing poses don't work for you](https://yogainternational.com/article/view/wrist-support-when-weight-bearing-poses-dont-work-for-you/). About 50 degrees wrist extension in dog vs about 90 in hand balances; who should modify and how.
- [Yoga International, Doug Keller: Healing (and preventing) wrist injuries](https://yogainternational.com/article/view/healing-and-preventing-wrist-injuries). Press through the finger mounds; wedge, blanket and chair options for downward dog.
- [Yoga International, Karen Shelley: Two variations on downward-facing dog](https://yogainternational.com/article/view/old-dog-new-tricks-two-variations-on-downward-facing-dog). Strap-supported dog to reduce shoulder strain; restorative dog held 8 to 20 breaths.
- [YogaUOnline: The weight distribution secret inside downward dog that protects your wrists](https://yogauonline.com/yoga-practice-teaching-tips/yoga-teaching/the-weight-distribution-secret-inside-downward-facing-dog-pose-that-protects-your-wrists/). Keep weight out of the heel of the hand; blocks for sensitive wrists.
- [Yoga Journal, May 2018: the science of jumping back to chaturanga (via Magzter, excerpt only)](https://www.magzter.com/articles/6166/275979/5adee7cceae86). Links jump-backs with shoulder and wrist injuries; the excerpt cuts off before the findings.
- [Physiopedia: Yoga](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Yoga). General evidence summary; notes yoga research is often criticised for weak methods. No pose-specific safety content.
- [Wikipedia: Chaturanga Dandasana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturanga_Dandasana). Right-angle elbows close to the body; done on an exhalation in Ashtanga; knees-down beginner option. Reference summary, not a professional source.
- [Wikipedia: Downward Dog Pose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downward_Dog_Pose). One-leg-raised variation, chair version for shoulder injuries, summary of the Polis 2015 pregnancy study. Reference summary, not a professional source.
