## Pattern clarification

Sources do not agree on what "3/4" means. There are two readings:

1. **Partial ascent (GymVisual, FitnessVolt).** Lie supine with knees bent and feet flat. Curl up until the trunk is about three-quarters of the way to upright, then lower all the way back to the floor. FitnessVolt's guide (fitnessvolt.com/3-4-sit-up-guide) says "three-quarters of the way up, forming a V shape," with "hands gently behind your head, keeping your elbows wide," and gives no anchoring.
2. **Partial descent (Bodybuilding.com, iFitness.vn).** Bodybuilding.com (bodybuilding.com/exercises/34-sit-up) says: "secure your feet." The torso comes up to "perpendicular to the ground," then you go down "only ¾ of the way" so the back never rests on the floor. The iFitness.vn description repeats this setup (feet hooked, torso perpendicular at the top).

Fitwill (fitwill.app/exercise/2476/34-sit-up) is a third outlier. It stops "when your shoulder blades are just off the ground," which is really a crunch. Do not follow it.

**For our animation, follow reading 1, which matches the GymVisual reference:**

- **End range that defines "3/4":** the trunk rises to about 60-70 degrees from the floor (a V with the thighs), short of vertical. The lumbar spine stays mostly in contact as the curl begins, and the hips stay down.
- **Hand position:** fingertips lightly behind the head, elbows wide and kept wide through the rep. The hands do not pull the head forward, and the chin stays off the chest.
- **Feet:** flat on the floor, hip-width, knees about 90 degrees, **not anchored**. If a demo hooks the feet (reading 2), treat it as a variant.
- **Tempo:** no source gives counts. FitnessVolt asks for "slow and controlled," a brief pause at the top, a slow descent, exhaling on the way up and inhaling on the way down. Suggested animation timing (our recommendation, not from a source): about 1.5-2 s up, about 0.5 s hold, about 2-2.5 s down, with the head and shoulders touching down last.

## Confidence

Low to moderate. All four URLs are real and their titles and channels were confirmed. None could be watched, though, and no clinician source was found. Before using any of them for timing, view the My PT Hub and Get Right Results clips to check the end angle and whether the feet are free. The written pattern above is reasonably reliable (moderate) for reading 1.
