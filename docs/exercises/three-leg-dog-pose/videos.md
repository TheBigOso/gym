## Pattern clarification

Sources: Yoga Journal "Back Basics: Three-Legged Downward Dog Dissected" (Tiffany Russo) and the Everyday Yoga / Yoga Outlet how-to guide; video cues not verified (see method note).

- **Hips square vs open:** GymVisual shows the square version, and that is the one to animate. Keep both hip points facing the floor, pelvis level, lifted leg internally rotated slightly (toes point down, not out). Yoga Journal lists "opening the hip" and letting the glute rotate the leg outward as common mistakes. The open-hip variant (lifted knee bends, hip stacks over the standing hip, foot reaches across the back) is a separate, named variation; do not blend the two.
- **Leg height:** No fixed angle is given. Lift from the hip socket to roughly in line with the torso, or higher only if the pelvis stays level and the low back does not arch. Lifted foot flexed, heel reaching back. Standing heel reaches toward the floor but need not touch; standing knee may soften if hamstrings are tight.
- **Hold time:** In a flow (sun salutations) it often lasts one breath. As a held stretch, guides give 5 to 20 breaths per side. For the app, suggest 3 to 5 breaths per side for beginners, or slow lift-and-lower reps to match the GymVisual flow.
- **Breath cues:** Inhale as the leg lifts; exhale as it lowers back to down dog (or sweeps forward between the hands). Keep weight even in both hands and avoid sinking into the standing-leg side.
- **Other alignment:** Press evenly through the index-finger knuckles, keep side waists long, shoulders square to the top of the mat.
