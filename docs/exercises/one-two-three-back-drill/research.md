# 123 Back Drill: research

Researched 2026-11-26 for issue #13 (Batch 1, exercise 3 of 10). GymVisual reference #598413: three cones in a triangle, start at the back (apex) cone, sprint to a front cone, shuffle across, backpedal back to the apex, repeat.

**Evidence note.** No peer-reviewed study tests the "123 Back Drill" by name. Everything below is drawn from the closest studied category: planned (closed) change-of-direction (COD) cone drills that mix a forward sprint, a lateral shuffle and a backpedal, of which the Agility T-test is the best-studied example. Treat all drill-specific numbers as borrowed from that category. Tags: **[trial evidence]** = measured in a study or review of studies; **[clinical opinion]** = coaching or professional guidance, or our own reasoning, without direct trial data.

**Pattern caveat.** Sources disagree on what the drill is. The GymVisual reference shows a triangle with sprint, shuffle and backpedal legs. Fitwill's page for the same GymVisual exercise number describes three markers in a straight line with three short backward steps. We follow the GymVisual triangle (it matches the reference animation), but the video ticket should confirm the cone order and which leg is the shuffle versus the backpedal.

## Exercise card

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Agility / change-of-direction drill (GymVisual data sheet: "Aerobic", body part "Plyometrics") |
| Body part | Legs (quadriceps, glutes, calves) and hips, with trunk control |
| Equipment | Body weight plus 3 cones. Suggested spacing: a triangle with about 5 yd (4.57 m) sides. The 5 yd figure is borrowed from the T-test's lateral legs and the NFL three-cone drill, not from a source on this drill [clinical opinion]. Shorter sides (3 m) for beginners or small spaces. Non-slip, consistent surface. |
| Difficulty | Intermediate (beginners can do it at walking/jog pace first) |
| Mechanics | Compound, whole-body locomotion |
| Plane | Multiplanar: sagittal (sprint and backpedal) plus frontal (lateral shuffle), with transverse turns at each cone |
| Laterality | Bilateral, alternating limbs; the turning direction matters, so run the circuit both ways (clockwise and counterclockwise) |

### Muscles

- **Target**
  - Quadriceps (vasti): the main braking muscles when decelerating at each cone. Eccentric knee-extensor strength is associated with deceleration ability, and vasti activity can exceed 150% of a maximal isometric contraction during braking [trial evidence, Harper 2022 review].
  - Gluteus maximus: hip extension during the sprint push-off and in the low braking position [clinical opinion; no drill-specific EMG found].
- **Synergist**
  - Gluteus medius / hip abductors and hip adductors: drive and catch the lateral shuffle [clinical opinion; no shuffle EMG study was fetched].
  - Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus): push-off on the balls of the feet in all three segments [clinical opinion].
  - Hamstrings: hip extension in the sprint; during braking they appear to work largely isometrically, and their link to deceleration ability is weaker than the quadriceps' [trial evidence, Harper 2022, which calls for more research].
- **Stabilizer**
  - Tibialis anterior and the trailing limb: help the leading limb brake [trial evidence, Harper 2022].
  - Trunk (abdominals, obliques, spinal extensors): hold posture through braking and turns; poor trunk position (excess side lean) is a recognised risk factor in COD [trial evidence, McBurnie 2021 review].
  - Hip abductors / external rotators: keep the knee over the foot on plants [clinical opinion, consistent with the knee-valgus findings below].

### Posture and movement norms

| Phase | Norm | Tag and source |
|---|---|---|
| Start / athletic stance | Feet about hip width, knees soft, chest up, weight on the balls of the feet, eyes forward | [clinical opinion] Fitwill |
| Sprint (apex to first cone) | Forward lean of roughly 45° in early acceleration, foot landing under the hips with a positive shin angle, no overstriding, arms driven back in time with the steps | [clinical opinion] Turner 2026 (SCJ coaching article) |
| Deceleration into a cone | Brake over several short steps, not one. Lower the centre of mass with hip, knee and ankle flexion (knee up to about 100° on the penultimate step); place the braking foot ahead of the body; lean the trunk back slightly; penultimate-step contact roughly 0.15-0.40 s | [trial evidence, review of studies] Dos'Santos 2019 (penultimate foot contact) |
| Braking forces | Peak braking force arrives within about 50 ms of contact and can be about 2.7 times early-acceleration force, so landings must be controlled | [trial evidence] Harper 2022 |
| Plant / final step | Plant in front of the body, in line with the new direction, closer to the midline, trunk upright; avoid doing all the braking on this last step | [trial evidence, review] Dos'Santos 2019 |
| Knee alignment | Knee tracks over the toes; avoid the knee collapsing inward (valgus), which raises knee loading and ACL strain | [trial evidence] McBurnie 2021; Dos'Santos 2019 |
| Lateral shuffle | Hips and chest face forward (the T-test rule), feet never cross, low "set" position between cones | [clinical opinion] T-test protocol (Topend Sports, Physiopedia); Turner 2026 |
| Backpedal | Stay low with weight over the balls of the feet, shoulders slightly ahead of the hips, short quick steps; do not lean back or sit into the heels | [clinical opinion] Fitwill |
| Angle and speed | Faster entries make the intended turn harder and raise knee loads, so speed should be progressed only as control allows | [trial evidence, review] Dos'Santos 2018 (angle-velocity trade-off) |

### Breathing

Breathe naturally and rhythmically through each repetition; do not hold the breath on the plants. Use the rest between reps to recover with slow nasal or relaxed mouth breathing. [clinical opinion; no study on breathing in agility drills was found]

### Prescription

- **Work:** one triangle circuit takes roughly 5-10 s at 5 yd sides. Estimate only, by analogy with the full T-test (36.56 m, four 90° turns), which takes about 7.5-13 s [trial evidence for the T-test time range, Nimphius 2018; the estimate for this drill is clinical opinion].
- **Reps:** 4-8 circuits per set, half clockwise and half counterclockwise; 2-4 sets [clinical opinion]. Train braking on both limbs, as asymmetries can reduce performance and raise injury risk [trial evidence, Harper 2022 review].
- **Rest:** full recovery. NSCA guidance: rest between sets should not be shorter than two minutes unless the work bout is very short [clinical opinion, NSCA Developing Agility and Quickness]. In practice, about 20-40 s between circuits and 2-3 min between sets [clinical opinion].
- **Intensity:** start new drills at slower speeds and move to full speed only once technique is clean [clinical opinion, NSCA]. Use it as a quickness/agility drill, not as conditioning [clinical opinion, NSCA].
- **Frequency:** 1-3 times per week, early in the session after a warm-up, while fresh [clinical opinion].
- **Progression:** walk-through, then jog, then full speed; then reactive versions where a partner calls the next cone ("switch", "back"), keeping choices to two or three [clinical opinion, NSCA COD speed drill adaptation].
- **Why do it:** agility drills consistently improved COD performance in the studies NSCA reviewed, whereas strength training alone did not [clinical opinion summarising trials, NSCA Guide to Program Design]. COD technique training is among the most effective ways to reduce knee loading in cutting [trial evidence, Dos'Santos 2019 scoping review].

### Cues

1. "Start in an athletic stance: soft knees, weight on the balls of your feet."
2. "Drive out low and push the ground back; arms pump."
3. "Chop your feet to slow down: several short steps, hips sink, before you reach the cone."
4. "Knee over toes on every plant; don't let it cave in."
5. "Shuffle with hips and chest facing forward; feet never cross or click."
6. "Backpedal low and light: shoulders over your toes, short quick steps."
7. "Stay low at every cone; don't stand up between segments."

### Common mistakes and fixes

| Mistake | Fix | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Braking in one big last step (straight-legged plant) | Start slowing earlier with 2-3 short steps; sink the hips on the penultimate step | [trial evidence] Dos'Santos 2019 |
| Knee caving inward on the plant | Slow down and plant with the knee over the second toe; widen the base slightly | [trial evidence] McBurnie 2021; Dos'Santos 2019 |
| Standing tall at the cones | Keep hips low through the turn; "head never goes between the toes" | [clinical opinion] Turner 2026 |
| Crossing the feet or turning the hips during the shuffle | Push off the trail leg, feet stay apart, chest stays forward | [clinical opinion] T-test rules |
| Leaning back or sitting on the heels during the backpedal | Shoulders over the toes, weight on the balls of the feet, short steps | [clinical opinion] Fitwill |
| Excess sideways trunk lean on turns | Keep the trunk stacked over the hips; lower the speed until control returns | [trial evidence] McBurnie 2021 |
| Only turning one way | Alternate clockwise and counterclockwise so both legs brake | [trial evidence, review] Harper 2022 |

### Safety and who should modify

- **Knee injury or ACL history:** non-contact ACL injuries are associated with deceleration and direction changes, especially at the final plant [trial evidence, McBurnie 2021; Dos'Santos 2019]. Reduce speed and turn sharpness first, then progress, as the angle-velocity framework recommends for rehabilitating athletes [trial evidence, review, Dos'Santos 2018]. Follow a clinician's return-to-sport plan.
- **Patellofemoral (front-of-knee) pain:** in 20 healthy runners, backward running produced lower patellofemoral compression than forward running (about 3.4 vs 4.5 body weights), though results varied by person [trial evidence, Roos 2012]. Backpedal segments may be tolerated better than braking-heavy sprint segments; this is indirect evidence for this drill.
- **Balance problems, older adults, pregnancy:** backpedalling raises fall risk. Use walking pace, a short triangle and no backpedal (turn and walk instead) [clinical opinion]. The T-test protocol recommends a spotter behind the backpedal finish to catch athletes who fall [clinical opinion, Topend Sports].
- **Ankle sprain history:** start with jog pace and shorter sides; build ankle and calf strength [clinical opinion].
- **Strength base:** adequate eccentric strength is needed to tolerate braking forces [trial evidence, review, Dos'Santos 2019; Harper 2022].
- **Surface and footwear:** dry, non-slip surface, consistent between sessions; supportive court/field shoes [clinical opinion].
- **Stop if:** sharp joint pain, the knee gives way, or form breaks down (steps lose rhythm, torso rocks back) [clinical opinion].

## 1. Scientific studies

- [Harper et al. 2022, Biomechanical and neuromuscular requirements of horizontal deceleration (Sports Med)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01693-0). Review: braking forces peak early and high; eccentric quadriceps strength is linked to deceleration; lower, more posterior centre of mass helps braking.
- [McBurnie et al. 2021, Deceleration training in team sports: another potential "vaccine" for injury? (Sports Med, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8761154). Current-opinion review (no new data): deceleration is a non-contact ACL mechanism; low centre of mass and triple flexion are key technique points.
- [Dos'Santos et al. 2018, Angle-velocity trade-off in change of direction (Sports Med)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-018-0968-3). Review: faster entries compromise the intended turn and raise knee loading; progress angle and speed carefully.
- [Dos'Santos et al. 2019, Training interventions and COD biomechanics linked to ACL loading: scoping review (Sports Med)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-019-01171-0). 22 studies: balance training and COD technique modification reduced knee loading most; resistance training and several warm-ups did not.
- [Dos'Santos et al. 2019, Role of the penultimate foot contact during change of direction (Strength Cond J, Salford repository)](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1368035/role-of-the-penultimate-foot-contact-during-change-of-direction-implications-on-performance-and-risk-of-injury). Review: the step before the plant is a key "preparatory step" for braking turns of 60° or more.
- [Same review, author manuscript PDF with coaching guidelines (MMU e-space)](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/626016/3/The%20role%20of%20the%20PFC%20-%20R2.pdf). Technique table: foot ahead of the body, knee flexion up to about 100°, trunk back, avoid knee valgus, brake over several steps.
- [Sassi et al. 2009, Reliability of a modified agility T-test (J Strength Cond Res, PDF)](https://squ.elsevierpure.com/files/89163639/JSCR_2009.pdf). 86 students: a 20 m sprint-shuffle-backpedal T-test was highly reliable (ICC 0.92 women, 0.95 men).
- [Nimphius et al. 2018, Change of direction and agility tests: challenging our current measures (Strength Cond J, NSCA PDF)](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/contentassets/adc4a284b84f4b998257155a47d0dcc3/change_of_direction_and_agility_tests__challenging.4.pdf). T-test takes about 7.5-13 s; total time can hide poor turning behind fast linear speed.
- [Roos et al. 2012, Patellofemoral compression in backward and forward running (J Biomech, Cardiff ORCA)](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/23555/). 20 runners: backward running gave lower patellofemoral compression than forward running.
- [Turner 2026, Training agility over 60-180° turns (Strength Cond J, NSCA PDF)](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/certification/rscc/2026-renewal/scj--cod-and-agility.pdf). Coaching mechanics article (not a trial): base of support, centre of mass and force direction in turns and shuffles.

Not directly fetched: Pauole et al. 2000 (J Strength Cond Res 14(4):443-450), the original T-test reliability study. Its figures (test-retest r = 0.98 men, 0.90 women) are quoted here only as reported by Topend Sports below.

## 2. Clinical and professional articles

- [Physiopedia, Agility T-Test](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Agility_T-Test). Sprint-shuffle-backpedal layout, invalid-trial rules, norms; cites Pauole 2000 calling it "highly reliable".
- [Topend Sports, T-Test](https://topendsports.com/testing/tests/t-test.htm). Same protocol; spotter behind the backpedal; reports Pauole 2000 reliability (r = 0.98 men, 0.90 women).
- [NSCA, Intensity and volume by drill type](https://nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/intensity-and-volume-by-drill-type). Volume as total work time; rest of at least two minutes between sets; start new drills slow.
- [NSCA, Change of direction speed drill adaptation](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/change-of-direction-speed-drill-adaptation). Progression from closed drills to reactive cues (two or three options), including a "back" call to backpedal.
- [NSCA, Transfer of training for agility](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/transfer-of-training-for-agility/). Summary of trials: agility drills improve COD; strength training alone mostly does not.
- [NSCA, Assessing agility using the T-test, 5-10-5 shuttle and Illinois test](https://nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/assessing-agility-using-the-t-test-5-10-5-shuttle-and-illinois-test). Confirms the T-test as a standard lateral plus front-to-back agility test.
- [Wikipedia, Three-cone drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-cone). NFL combine drill with three cones 5 yd apart; supports the 5 yd spacing convention (different pattern from this drill).
- [Fitwill, 123 Back Drill](https://fitwill.app/exercise/5984/123-back-drill.md). App page for the same GymVisual exercise; describes a straight-line backward variant (conflicts with the triangle reference) and gives backpedal posture cues.

Evidence gaps: no study of this exact drill; no fetched EMG data for lateral shuffles or backpedalling, so the glute, adductor and calf roles above are clinical opinion; prescription numbers are coaching convention.
