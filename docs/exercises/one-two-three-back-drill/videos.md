## Pattern clarification

The pattern below comes mainly from two written coaching sources, because no video description spelled out the full triangle:

- Physitrack clinical exercise "Agility: change of direction, acceleration/deceleration (side step, backwards, forwards, anticlockwise), triangle formation": three cones in a triangle; start at cone one facing the midpoint between cones two and three; go forwards 1 to 3, sidestep 3 to 2, go backwards 2 to 1, then repeat. (https://us.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/agility-%25e2%2580%2593-change-of-direction---acceleration-%252f-deceleration-%2528side-step%252c-backwards%252c-forwards-anticlockwise%2529-%25e2%2580%2593-triangle-formation)
- James Madison University strength and conditioning footwork/agility manual: a related 4-cone drill using the convention sprint forward, shuffle laterally (5 yards to each side), backpedal back to the start. (https://educ.jmu.edu/~strength/JMU_Summer_2000_WebPage/JMU_Summer_2000_Sections/11_Summer_Footwork_Agility.htm)

This matches the GymVisual reference: apex cone at the back, two front-corner cones, a 1-2-3 cone sequence with a backward segment.

**Cone layout and distances**
- Cone 1 (apex / start) is at the back. Cones 2 and 3 are the two front corners.
- The front cones are 5 yards (about 4.5 m) apart. Cone 1 sits about 5 yards behind the midpoint between them, forming a roughly isosceles triangle. A 5-yard spacing is the standard in the sources above (JMU's lateral cones, XCELER8's triangle). Exact distances are not shown in the GymVisual GIF.
- Seen from above with the athlete facing the front cones: cone 3 is front-right, cone 2 is front-left. Running the anticlockwise order from the apex, as Physitrack does, sends the athlete to the right-hand cone first.

**Order and segments (one lap)**
1. **Cone 1 to cone 3 (front-right): forward sprint**, diagonal, short sprint steps, arms pumping, deceleration at the cone.
2. **Cone 3 to cone 2 (front-left): lateral shuffle**, still facing forward, hips low, feet not crossing.
3. **Cone 2 to cone 1 (apex): backpedal**, diagonal, still facing forward, hips low, chest over the toes.

The athlete faces the same direction (toward the front cones) for the whole lap, so the body turns no corners. Only the direction of travel changes, which is what produces the forward, sideways and backward segments.

**Loop**
- On reaching cone 1, the athlete goes straight into the next lap (continuous loop, as in the GymVisual GIF).
- Coaching practice (not shown in the GIF) is to run sets in both directions: anticlockwise (sprint right, shuffle left, backpedal) and clockwise (sprint to cone 2, shuffle right to cone 3, backpedal to cone 1). For the animation, one direction looped seamlessly is enough. Anticlockwise is recommended to match the Physitrack source.

**Open point for the animation ticket:** in the low-resolution GIF, the reference says the athlete "shuffles/backpedals across or diagonally". This file assigns the shuffle to the front edge (cone 3 to cone 2) and the backpedal to the diagonal return (cone 2 to cone 1), as the Physitrack source does. That is the only layout that keeps the athlete facing forward and makes the "Back" in the name the final leg.
